How Percentages Work

A percentage expresses a quantity as a fraction of 100. The word “percent” literally means “per hundred.” For example, 25% represents 25 out of every 100, which is the same as 0.25 or one quarter.

Finding a Percentage of a Number

Convert the percentage to a decimal by dividing by 100, then multiply by the number. For example, 15% of 200 is 0.15 × 200 = 30.

Finding What Percent One Number Is of Another

Divide the first number by the second and multiply by 100. If 45 out of 180 items meet a condition, 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%.

Calculating Percentage Change

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide that difference by the absolute value of the original, then multiply by 100. A positive result represents an increase and a negative result represents a decrease. Percentage change is undefined when the original value is zero.

Working Backward from a Percentage Change

If a $120 price is 20% higher than its original price, divide $120 by 1.20 to get $100. If a value is 20% lower than the original, divide the final value by 0.80 instead.

Percentage Points vs. Percent Change

These terms are different. If a rate rises from 10% to 12%, it increased by 2 percentage points. Relative to the original 10% rate, however, the increase is 20%.

Percentages Above 100%

Percentages can exceed 100%. For example, 150% of 40 equals 60. A 100% increase means the original amount has doubled; a 200% increase means it has tripled.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate 10% quickly?

Move the decimal point one place to the left. Ten percent of 250 is 25.

How do I calculate a discount?

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage to find the discount amount, then subtract it from the original price.

How do I add a percentage to a number?

Multiply the original number by 1 plus the percentage written as a decimal. To add 8%, multiply by 1.08.

How do I subtract a percentage from a number?

Multiply the original number by 1 minus the percentage written as a decimal. To reduce a number by 15%, multiply it by 0.85.

Why can’t percentage change use zero as the original value?

The standard percentage-change formula divides by the original value. Division by zero is undefined, so a conventional percentage change cannot be calculated from an original value of zero.
