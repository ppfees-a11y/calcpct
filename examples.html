Worked examples: from question to answer

Use these walkthroughs to check your own calculations and understand the correct base.

Example 1: Sale price and tax

Question: A $160 jacket is 30% off, with 7% sales tax on the discounted price.

Discount: $160 × 0.30 = $48. Sale price: $160 − $48 = $112. Tax: $112 × 0.07 = $7.84. Final: $119.84.

Example 2: Raise versus salary

Question: A monthly salary changes from $4,000 to $4,300.

Difference: $300. Percent increase: $300 ÷ $4,000 × 100 = 7.5%.

Example 3: Reverse a discounted price

Question: The sale price is $68 after a 15% discount.

Remaining proportion: 0.85. Original = $68 ÷ 0.85 = $80.

Example 4: Profit margin versus markup

Question: An item costs $45 and sells for $75.

Profit = $30. Margin = $30 ÷ $75 = 40%. Markup = $30 ÷ $45 = 66.67%.

Example 5: Compounding a decrease

Question: A $500 value falls 10% in each of two periods.

After period one: $450. After period two: $405. Overall change: −$95 or −19%, not −20%.

Example 6: Percentage points

Question: A rate moves from 2% to 3%.

The increase is one percentage point, while the relative increase is (3−2) ÷ 2 × 100 = 50%.

Example 7: Tip and split

Question: A $96 bill has a 20% tip and is split among four people.

Tip = $19.20. Total = $115.20. Each share = $28.80.
